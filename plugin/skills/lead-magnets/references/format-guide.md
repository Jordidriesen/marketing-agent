# Lead Magnet Format Guide

Per-format creation detail: what it is, how to structure it, how long, which tool, and the common mistakes. Read the row for the format the main skill recommended.

## Checklist

**What it is:** a scannable list of steps or checks for one process. The lowest-effort magnet with strong perceived value.

**Structure:** a one-line promise → the checklist grouped into 2–4 phases → one "what good looks like" note per phase → a single next step (usually the product). 10–25 items.

**Length:** 1–2 pages. **Tool:** a designed PDF, or a copyable checklist in a doc.

**Mistakes:** items that are vague ("optimise your site") rather than checkable ("every page loads in under 2 seconds"); no grouping; padding to hit a round number.

## Cheat sheet

**What it is:** a dense one-page reference someone keeps open while working — commands, formulas, shortcuts, a decision tree, a glossary.

**Structure:** organised for lookup, not reading — columns, a table, or a labelled diagram. No narrative.

**Length:** 1 page, sometimes 2. **Tool:** designed PDF; landscape often works better.

**Mistakes:** treating it as a mini-guide with paragraphs; too much white space; needing a legend to read.

## Template (doc / spreadsheet / Notion)

**What it is:** a reusable file the person fills in — a brief template, a calculator sheet, a content calendar, a Notion database.

**Structure:** pre-filled with one worked example, then blank rows or sections; instructions in comments or a first "How to use" tab, not mixed into the working area; formulas locked where the user shouldn't edit.

**Length:** whatever the job needs. **Tool:** Google Sheets / Docs (share as "make a copy"), Notion (duplicate to workspace), or an .xlsx.

**Mistakes:** shipping it empty with no example; instructions cluttering the template; broken formulas; a format the audience doesn't use.

## Swipe file

**What it is:** a curated collection of real examples — subject lines, ad headlines, landing pages, cold emails — with a short note on why each works.

**Structure:** grouped by use case; each entry is the example verbatim plus 1–2 lines of analysis; a short "how to adapt this" at the end of each group.

**Length:** 15–40 examples. **Tool:** PDF or a Notion gallery.

**Mistakes:** examples with no annotation; made-up examples; no attribution; nothing the reader can act on.

## Ebook / guide

**What it is:** long-form education on a topic, positioning the author as the expert. High effort, slow to produce, strong for authority and SEO.

**Structure:** a promise and who it's for up front → 4–8 chapters that each solve one sub-problem → a worked example or case per chapter → a closing chapter on doing this at scale (where the product enters) → a one-page summary or checklist as an appendix.

**Length:** 15–40 pages. Draft it chapter by chapter, treating each chapter like a web article under `web-content-pipeline`'s writing-style rules, then run `copy-editing` and `ai-content-cleaner` across the whole.

**Tool:** designed PDF; a gated web page version helps SEO.

**Mistakes:** breadth over depth; no examples; a sales pitch from page one; a wall of text with no structure; shipping before an edit pass.

## Mini-course (email)

**What it is:** a 3–7 email sequence that teaches one skill over days, ending with a next step. Doubles as education and nurture.

**Structure:** one lesson per email, each with a single takeaway and one small action; a recap-and-next-lesson line; the final email transitions to the product or a call.

**Build:** the sequence itself is `email-sequence-hubspot-brevo`'s job — hand it the lesson outline, the cadence, and the exit condition.

**Mistakes:** lessons too long for an email; no action; the sales email arriving before value has landed.

## Mini-course (video)

**What it is:** the email mini-course with recorded video lessons. Adds personality and trust, much higher production cost.

**Structure:** 3–6 videos of 3–8 minutes, each with a worked demo; a one-page notes PDF per video; hosted on a gated page or delivered by drip email.

**Mistakes:** unscripted rambling; no captions; videos over 10 minutes; production quality that undercuts the brand.

## Quiz / assessment

**What it is:** a set of questions that returns a scored result and a tailored recommendation. Strong for segmentation and engagement.

**Structure:** 5–12 questions → a result type or score band → a specific recommendation per band → email gate before the full result → segment the contact by result for the nurture.

**Build:** this is an interactive build — route to `free-tool-strategy` for the tool itself; this skill supplies the question logic, the result bands, and the segmentation plan.

**Mistakes:** results that are all flattering and interchangeable; too many questions; no reason to enter an email; not using the result downstream.

## Webinar

**What it is:** a live or evergreen session — teaching, a demo, or a panel — with registration as the capture point.

**Structure:** a specific outcome-focused title → 20–30 minutes of teaching with no pitch → 5–10 minutes on how the product does this → live Q&A → a time-boxed offer. Register, remind (3 emails), attend, replay, follow up.

**Mistakes:** a vague title; pitching throughout; no replay for no-shows; no follow-up sequence.

## Resource library

**What it is:** an ongoing gated hub of multiple assets. High cumulative effort, good for return visits and a single reusable capture point.

**Structure:** one gate for the whole library; assets grouped by topic or buyer stage; new assets added on a schedule and announced to the list.

**Mistakes:** launching with too little in it; no update cadence; gating individual items as well as the library.
