# Battlecard Template

A competitive battlecard is a separate artifact from the brief itself — a one-page reference for sales, built from the brief's findings once they exist. Build one when asked, not by default; it's an optional Step 8, not part of every competitive-brief run.

## Structure

**Header** — competitor name, last updated date, competitive win rate if tracked.

**Quick overview** — what they do in one sentence, target customer, pricing model summary, key recent developments.

**Their pitch** — how they describe themselves, primary tagline, top 3 claimed differentiators.

**Strengths (be honest)** — where they genuinely compete well, what customers like about them per reviews, capabilities where they lead. A battlecard that only lists weaknesses gets ignored by sales the first time a prospect pushes back with something true.

**Weaknesses** — consistent customer complaints from reviews, technical limitations, offering gaps.

**Our differentiators** — 3–5 specific ways the product or approach differs, each with why it matters to the customer and proof.

**Objection handling**

| If the prospect says... | Respond with... |
|---|---|
| "[Competitor] does X too" | How the approach genuinely differs |
| "[Competitor] is cheaper" | What the price difference gets you |
| "I've heard good things about [Competitor]" | Where they're strong, where you differ |

**Landmines to set** — questions to ask prospects early that surface an advantage: "How do you currently handle [area where the competitor is weak]?", "How important is [capability you have that they lack]?"

**Landmines to defuse** — questions competitors might prime prospects to ask, with prepared responses ready before they come up.

**Win/loss themes** — common reasons deals are won or lost against this competitor, and what type of prospect tends to favor each side.

**Maintenance** — review quarterly at minimum, update immediately after major competitor announcements, fold in win/loss feedback from sales, and track which objection-handling responses actually work in the field.
