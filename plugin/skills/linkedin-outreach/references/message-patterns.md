# Message patterns

Structures to fill with real, supplied material. Nothing here is a claim, an example to send as
written or a statistic.

## Connection note

Shape: who I am in a few words, why you, no ask.

```
Hi {first_name}, [a specific reason, drawn from something supplied: a post, a project, a shared
event or group]. [One short clause on why that caught my attention.] Would be good to be connected.
```

Keep it well under the platform's current character limit. If there is no real reason, send no note.

## First message after they accept

Shape: thanks, one relevant observation, one easy question.

```
Thanks for connecting, {first_name}.
[One specific observation about their work or situation, from supplied material.]
[One easy question they can answer in a line, about their situation and not about our product.]
```

No pitch. No link. If they answer, the conversation starts there.

## Follow-up (a respectful gap later)

Shape: a different angle or something useful.

```
{first_name}, [a new, relevant piece of information, or a short resource that fits what they do].
No need to reply. [Optional: one low-pressure question.]
```

Never "just checking in" or "bumping this". If there is nothing useful to add, do not follow up.

## Comment-first warm-up

Shape: a comment the sender would leave anyway.

- Add something real: a related experience, a fair question, a respectful point of difference.
- No pitch, no link, no "great post!".
- Leave it a few days before the connection note, so the note lands on a familiar name.

## InMail

Shape: short, a clear reason, a small ask.

```
Subject: [plain, specific, matches the body]

{first_name}, [the reason I am writing, specific to you]. [What I think could be useful, in one line.]
[One small ask: a question, or fifteen minutes only if there is a clear reason.]
```

## Replies

| Reply | Approach |
|---|---|
| Positive | Reply promptly, suggest two time slots, confirm the goal in one line |
| Curious | Answer in two or three lines, ask one question |
| Not now | Thank them, agree when to reconnect, set a reminder, stop |
| Objection | Acknowledge, ask one fair question, do not argue |
| Wrong person | Thank them, ask who is right, stop |
| "No" or silence after a follow-up | Thank them if they replied, stop and do not message again |

## Pacing

Gaps between messages are working-day starting points the sender can change. Stop at any reply.
Messaging many people quickly is a platform risk and a courtesy problem: the sender sends by hand,
at a pace a person can keep, and reads the platform's current limits.
