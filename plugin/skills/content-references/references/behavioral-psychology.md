# Behavioral Science Playbook for Copy & Visual Assets

Condensed from two research passes: Richard Shotton's trilogy (*The Choice
Factory*, *The Illusion of Choice*, *Hacking the Human Mind*) plus the wider
evidence base around it — Ehrenberg-Bass Institute (Byron Sharp, Jenni
Romaniuk), Les Binet & Peter Field, Orlando Wood/System1, Robert Cialdini,
the direct-response classics (Ogilvy, Caples, Schwartz), and processing-
fluency research.

Part of the `content-references` shared library — see
`content-references/SKILL.md` for how generation skills should use this
alongside the other four modules.

## How to use this file

Don't apply every row to every piece of content. Cross-reference against
`content-references/references/content-intent-framework.md` — each row
below is tagged with the intent type(s) it matters most for. Pull only what's
relevant.

**Non-negotiable across every principle below:** these apply to *true*
claims only. Precision and specificity mean citing a real number, never
inventing one that sounds plausible. This playbook never overrides the
no-fabrication rules already in `web-content-pipeline` and `customer-story-writer`.

## Copy principles

| Principle | What it does | How to apply | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Precision over round numbers** | Precise, odd numbers anchor more firmly and read as more credible than round ones (Janiszewski & Uy, 2008) | "37% faster," "2,847 teams" — not "about a third faster," "thousands of teams" | All, esp. Transactional |
| **Anchoring / price relativity** | The first number or comparison shown reframes everything that follows | Lead pricing/comparison pages with the reference point you want remembered | Transactional |
| **Charm pricing & extremeness aversion** | Prices ending .99 feel cheaper; adding a premium tier makes the middle tier the obvious choice | Use on pricing pages and tiered offers | Transactional |
| **Framing** | Identical facts land differently depending on frame ("sold out" reads as popular; "unavailable" reads as your failure) | Audit *every* system-generated or fallback message for framing, not just hero copy — error states, empty states, out-of-stock notices | Transactional, Navigational (error states) |
| **Curse of knowledge** | Experts systematically overestimate what's obvious to a stranger | Strip jargon; have someone outside the category read the draft before it ships | Informational |
| **Concreteness / specificity** | Specific, imagery-evoking language is dramatically more memorable than abstraction | Replace "improve your workflow" with the exact before/after state | All |
| **Pratfall effect** | A small, honest admitted flaw from an already-competent source increases likeability and trust (Aronson) | One controlled, real caveat, placed once competence is established — never on a first touchpoint before trust exists | Transactional (late-page), About pages, reviews |
| **Social proof — framed correctly** | People copy others' behavior, but a *negatively* framed norm can backfire and normalize the unwanted behavior (Cialdini's Petrified Forest field study) | State the desired norm positively; never write copy that implies "most people don't do X" | Transactional |
| **Reactance reduction ("but you are free")** | Explicitly stating the reader is free to decline paradoxically increases compliance | "No obligation," "cancel anytime" placed near a firm CTA | Transactional |
| **Rhyme-as-reason / phonological fluency** | Rhyming or phonologically smooth phrases are judged truer (McGlone & Tofighbakhsh) | Worth testing on taglines and headlines — don't force it, and don't overuse it | Informational headlines, brand lines |
| **Cognitive / processing fluency** | Easy-to-read text and layout is judged more trustworthy and more true, independent of the argument inside it (Reber & Schwarz) | Short sentences, plain words, high visual contrast — the single highest-leverage lever across every content type | All |
| **Peak-end rule** | People judge an experience by its emotional peak and its ending, not the average (Kahneman) | End pages and posts on the strongest point and the easiest next step — never a flat feature recap | All |
| **Primacy / recency** | The first and last items in a sequence are best remembered | Strongest benefit first *and* last on a page; strongest bullet first and last in a list | Transactional |

## Visual principles

| Principle | What it does | How to apply | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Distinctive brand assets** (Ehrenberg-Bass) | Consistent non-name cues — colour, shape, character, layout — build recognition without needing the logo | Reuse the same colour/shape/iconography system across every asset; resist redesigning per campaign | Navigational, Transactional |
| **Broad-beam, human-centred creative** (Orlando Wood / System1) | Living characters, story, and humour outperform flat, isolated "narrow attention" feature shots for long-term effectiveness | Favor human/contextual imagery over isolated product shots where the format allows | Informational, brand/awareness content |
| **Watching-eyes / face cues** | Images of eyes or faces increase trust and prosocial compliance in some studies (contested — treat as low-cost, unproven upside, not a guarantee) | Consider near testimonials or trust-sensitive CTAs; test rather than assume | Transactional |
| **Picture superiority / dual coding** | Images paired with text are remembered far better than either alone (Paivio) | Pair key concepts with a real diagram or photo, never decorative stock | Informational |
| **Von Restorff / isolation effect** | The one visually distinct element in a layout is the one that gets noticed and remembered | Visually isolate the CTA or the single most important number — colour, whitespace, size | Transactional |
| **Figure-ground contrast & typography fluency** | High contrast and legible type read as more trustworthy and true | Never trade contrast or legibility for aesthetics on any conversion-relevant page | All |

## Caveats to carry into every application

- Several effects here rest on small or contested studies (the pique effect
  raises response *rate* but not amount given; watching-eyes has documented
  replication failures). Treat these as hypotheses worth testing in Jordi's
  own campaigns, not guaranteed lifts — this mirrors Shotton's own chapter
  on the replicability crisis.
- Byron Sharp's standing critique of the "nudge" school: distinctiveness and
  mental/physical availability (Ehrenberg-Bass) are the strategic
  foundation; the biases above are execution-level sharpening on top of
  that foundation, not a replacement for reach and consistent brand assets.
- Binet & Field's effectiveness data argues for roughly 60% brand-building
  (broad, emotional) to 40% activation (narrow, rational) spend for
  long-run growth — keep that balance in mind when a piece leans heavily on
  the Transactional-column tactics above.
