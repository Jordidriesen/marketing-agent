# Positioning and Content-Gap Analysis Frameworks

Supporting methodology for `competitive-brief`'s Messaging Analysis, Content Gap Analysis, and Opportunities sections.

## Messaging comparison

**Value proposition** — for each competitor, document the promise (what they claim the customer achieves), the evidence (data, testimonials, demos backing the promise), the mechanism (how the product delivers it), and the claimed uniqueness (what only they claim to do).

**Narrative analysis** — reverse-engineer each competitor's story arc: the villain (status quo, legacy tools, complexity — what they position against), the hero (customer, product, or team), the transformation promised (before/after), and the stakes (what happens if the reader doesn't act). This is the same villain/hero/transformation structure `content-references/references/communication-frameworks.md` describes for StoryBrand — here it's applied diagnostically, to read a competitor's positioning rather than to write your own.

**Strengths and vulnerabilities** — for each competitor's messaging, assess clarity (can a first-time visitor understand what they do in 5 seconds?), differentiation (distinct or generic?), proof (are claims backed by evidence?), consistency (same message across channels?), and resonance (does it address real pain points?).

## Content gap analysis

Map content across competitors:

| Topic/Theme | Own content | Competitor A | Competitor B | Gap? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Topic] | Blog post, ebook | Blog series, webinar | Nothing | Opportunity for B |

And by format:

| Format | You | Comp A | Comp B | Comp C |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blog posts | Y | Y | Y | Y |
| Case studies | Y | Y | N | Y |
| Ebooks/whitepapers | N | Y | Y | N |
| Webinars | Y | Y | Y | N |
| Podcast | N | N | Y | N |
| Video | N | Y | Y | Y |
| Interactive tools | N | N | N | Y |
| Templates/resources | Y | N | Y | N |

Look for: topics they cover that you don't (gap in your strategy), topics you cover that they don't (differentiator to amplify), formats they use that you don't (reach gap), audience segments they address that you don't, and search terms they rank for that you don't. Assess quality on top of coverage — depth, freshness, engagement, production value, and whether it's original insight or rehashed content — a coverage gap against a shallow competitor page is a much easier opportunity than one against a genuinely strong piece.

## Positioning strategy

**Positioning statement** — for your company and each competitor, define or reverse-engineer: "For [audience], [product] is the [category] that [key benefit] because [reason to believe]." Example: "For mid-market SaaS marketing teams, Acme is the campaign management platform that unifies planning and execution because it's built on a single data model that eliminates tool fragmentation."

**Positioning map** — plot competitors on a 2x2 using the two dimensions that matter most for this market: price vs. capability, ease of use vs. power, SMB vs. enterprise focus, point solution vs. platform, or innovative vs. established. Identify which quadrant is underserved.

**Category strategy** — four options, in order of risk: create a new category (high risk, high reward, requires genuinely different substance), reframe the existing category (change how buyers evaluate it to favor your strengths), win the existing category (compete directly, out-execute), or niche within it (own a specific segment or use case).

**Positioning pitfalls** — positioning against a competitor instead of for a customer need, claiming more than 1–2 differentiators, using category jargon the customer doesn't use, positioning on features instead of outcomes, and changing positioning too often.
