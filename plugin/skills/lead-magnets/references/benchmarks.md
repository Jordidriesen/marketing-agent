# Lead Magnet Benchmarks

Directional ranges, not promises. Use them to set targets and to spot when something is clearly off, then replace with the account's own numbers once there's data. State that these are industry ranges whenever you cite them.

## Landing page conversion rate (visitor → email)

| Traffic source | Typical range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Content upgrade (in-context, post-specific) | 20–45% | The highest-converting placement; intent is already established |
| Warm traffic (email list, retargeting, site regulars) | 20–40% | |
| Organic search to a dedicated landing page | 10–25% | Depends heavily on query intent match |
| Cold social (organic) | 5–15% | |
| Cold paid (lead ads, search) | 5–15% | Lead ads sit at the top of this range, search ads at the bottom for broad terms |
| Sidebar / footer / generic site-wide CTA | 1–3% | Low intent; treat as a floor, not a goal |

Format nudges this: a template or checklist tends to out-convert an ebook on the same topic and traffic, because the effort-to-payoff read is better.

## Cost per lead (paid)

Too channel- and market-dependent for a universal number. Rough B2B European ranges as a starting point only:

| Channel | Indicative CPL | |
|---|---|---|
| Meta lead ads (top-funnel magnet) | low tens of euros | Cheap leads, looser qualification |
| LinkedIn (B2B, seniority-targeted) | high tens to low hundreds of euros | Expensive, better fit |
| Google Search (high-intent: "X template", "X calculator") | mid tens of euros | Intent is strong; volume is limited |

Judge CPL against lead-to-opportunity rate, never on its own.

## Lead-to-customer / lead-to-opportunity

| Motion | Lead → opportunity | Lead → customer |
|---|---|---|
| B2B, sales-assisted | 3–10% | 1–5% |
| B2B, product-led (trial in the funnel) | — | 2–8% of leads start a trial; a fraction of those convert |
| B2C / low-ticket | — | often < 1%, offset by volume |

Decision-stage magnets (templates, ROI calculators, migration guides) convert to customer several times better than awareness-stage magnets, at lower volume. A healthy programme runs both.

## Email engagement on the delivery + nurture

| Metric | Healthy | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery email open | 45–65% | Below 35% suggests a mismatch between the promise and the audience, or a deliverability problem |
| Nurture open (emails 2–5) | 30–45% | Steep decline across the sequence means the lessons aren't landing |
| Nurture click | 2–6% | |
| Unsubscribe after delivery | under 1% per email | Over 2% means the magnet attracted the wrong people or the nurture pivots to selling too fast |
| Spam complaint rate | under 0.1% | Anything higher risks the sending domain |

## Time to conversion

Track median days from lead-magnet download to opportunity, split by magnet. Awareness magnets commonly sit at 60–180 days; decision magnets at 7–45. A magnet whose leads never progress, regardless of time, is attracting the wrong audience — change the topic or the gate, not the nurture.

## What "good" looks like overall

A lead magnet is working if, after 4–8 weeks: landing page conversion is within the range above for its traffic, cost per lead is at or below the channel indicative range, email engagement is in the healthy band, and at least a handful of leads have reached the next funnel stage at roughly the motion's expected rate. If conversion is fine but no leads progress, the problem is fit, not funnel.
